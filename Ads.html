The Psychology of Advertising

Chapter 1 Notes, Core Concepts & Psychological Approaches Hub

Foundations of Advertising & Consumer Responses

Advertising is defined as paid communication designed to inform and persuade consumers. In consumer psychology, responses are broken down into three core pillars:

  • Cognitive Responses: Thoughts, beliefs, and mental associations formed about a brand or product.
  • Affective Responses: Emotional reactions, moods, and feelings generated by the ad stimulus.
  • Behavioural Responses: Actual actions taken by the consumer, such as product trial, purchase, or brand switching.

Low vs. High Involvement: In low-involvement scenarios (like daily household items), first-time purchases rely on initial brand feel, after which habit and repeat purchase behavior take over naturally.

Integrated Marketing Communications & Promotional Mix

Companies do not rely solely on advertising. They utilize an integrated mix of communication tools:

  • Direct Marketing: Direct communication with targeted individual consumers via email, SMS, or personalized offers.
  • Sales Promotion: Short-term incentives like "Buy 1 Get 1 Free" or discounts to encourage immediate sales.
  • Public Relations (PR): Managing the overall public image and earned media coverage (often seen extensively in political and corporate realms).
  • Personal Selling: Two-way, face-to-face interaction between sales representatives and prospective buyers.
  • Interactive & Digital Marketing: Leveraging online platforms, social media, and digital targeting algorithms.

Approaches to Advertising Effectiveness

To measure whether advertising investments pay off, different eras and frameworks use distinct approaches:

  • The Naïve Approach: Assuming advertising works simply because it is ubiquitous and heavily budgeted.
  • The Economic Approach: Directly linking ad expenditure changes to fluctuations in sales volume.
  • The Media Approach: Measuring success based on media reach (how many eyes or ears encountered the ad).
  • The Psychological Approach: Examining individual-level cognitive, affective, and behavioral changes through rigorous scientific analysis.

Psychological Approach & Testing Methods

The psychological approach relies heavily on systematic experiments and data analysis:

  • Experimental Control: Manipulating independent variables (e.g., ad appeals, music, source credibility) to measure dependent attitude changes.
  • Mediation Analysis: Uncovering the internal psychological processes or thoughts that occur between the ad exposure and the final consumer decision.
  • Moderation Analysis: Testing boundary conditions, such as how consumer involvement or personal traits alter the strength of the advertising effect.

The Replication Crisis in Psychological Research

A critical modern perspective discussed in psychological research:

What is it? The replication crisis refers to the alarming discovery that many published psychological experiments cannot be successfully reproduced by independent researchers. This challenges the absolute certainty of behavioral claims made in marketing and advertising studies.
